Observation of long-range collective flow in OO and NeNe
collisions and implications for nuclear structure studies
The CMS Collaboration*

Abstract
The long-range collective flow of particles produced in oxygen-oxygen (OO) and
neon-neon (NeNe) collisions is measured with the CMS detector at the CERN LHC.
The data samples were collected at a center-of-mass energy per nucleon pair of
5.36 TeV, with integrated luminosities of 7 nb−1 and 0.8 nb−1 for OO and NeNe collisions, respectively. Two- and four-particle azimuthal correlations are measured over
nearly five units of pseudorapidity. Significant elliptic (v2 ) and triangular (v3 ) flow
harmonics are observed in both systems. The ratios of vn coefficients between NeNe
and OO collisions reveal sensitivity to quadrupole correlations in the nuclear wave
functions. Hydrodynamic models with ab initio nuclear structure inputs qualitatively
reproduce the collision-overlap dependence of both the vn values and the NeNe to
OO ratios. These measurements provide new constraints on hydrodynamic models
for small collision systems and offer valuable input on the nuclear structure of 16 O
and 20 Ne.

Quantum chromodynamics predicts that nuclear matter undergoes a phase transition to a deconfined state of quarks and gluons, known as the quark-gluon plasma (QGP), at extreme temperatures and densities [1, 2]. A key feature of QGP formation is the presence of collective flow,
which is characterized by strong correlations in the azimuthal (ϕ) distributions of particles produced over wide pseudorapidity (η) ranges in relativistic nucleus-nucleus collisions. These
correlations are quantified through a Fourier decomposition into flow harmonics (vn , where n
is the order of the harmonic), which map the hydrodynamic response of the QGP to the initial
collision geometry and its event-by-event fluctuations [3–5]. Experimental vn results at the BNL
RHIC [6–8] and CERN LHC [9–15] can be quantitatively described by hydrodynamic models
of a nearly perfect fluid [16–18].
Over the past decade, similar flow-like signals have been observed in high-multiplicity protonproton (pp) [19–23], proton-nucleus (pA) [15, 24–30], as well as in other collisions involving
a light ion with a heavy ion [31–40]. These observations raise the question of whether QGP
droplets are produced in small systems [41, 42]. Strong evidence of geometry-driven collective
flow has been found in pA collisions [43, 44]. A key challenge in quantitatively describing the
collective phenomena in pp and pA collisions is the strong dependence on the proton structure [40, 45]. In addition, pA collisions involve an asymmetric longitudinal geometry, further
complicating the theoretical modeling [46, 47]. In contrast, symmetric collisions of light ions
provide better control of the initial-state geometry, since it is determined primarily by the spatial distribution of nucleons rather than the partonic structure of the proton [48]. This makes
symmetric light ion collisions ideal for the investigation of the final-state collective response in
small collision systems.
Ultrarelativistic collisions of ions also offer a unique opportunity to probe nuclear structure.
Collective flow measurements are sensitive to spatial correlations in the nuclear ground state,
often described in terms of deformation effects [49–56]. Ion collisions provide a probe of the initial matter distribution, and have enabled extraction of the uranium nucleus quadrupole deformation [55]. The study of oxygen-oxygen (OO) and neon-neon (NeNe) collisions is particularly
compelling because the two ions have similar mass numbers, leading to expectation of similar
hydrodynamic evolution. Differences in their flow properties are therefore expected to primarily reflect differences in their nuclear structure. The 16 O and the 20 Ne nuclei are predicted to
have tetrahedral and ”bowling pin”-like intrinsic structures where nucleons may group into
α-cluster configurations [57–63], each with two paired neutrons and two paired protons.
Hydrodynamic simulations with ab initio nuclear structure inputs, including nuclear lattice effective field theory, variational Monte Carlo, and extended quantum molecular dynamics simulations [64–66], predict that the NeNe to OO elliptic flow (v2 ) ratio is directly sensitive to the
exotic deformation of 20 Ne [48]. Thus, OO and NeNe collisions provide a unique opportunity
not only to test to what extent collectivity in small systems is driven by initial geometry, but
also to study the nuclear structure of these light ions.
This Letter presents measurements of collective flow in OO and NeNe collisions at a center-of√
mass energy per nucleon pair of sNN = 5.36 TeV with the CMS detector at the LHC. The v2
and v3 harmonics are extracted from long-range, two-particle correlations over a large pseudorapidity range. The v2 values are also determined from four-particle correlations with a different response to event-by-event geometry fluctuations than two-particle measurements. The
harmonic values, measured for various overlap ranges, are compared to hydrodynamic models
incorporating modern understanding of nuclear structure in their initial conditions. Tabulated
results are provided in the HEPData record for this analysis [67]. During the final preparation of this Letter, the ATLAS and ALICE Collaborations submitted collective flow results in

The central feature of the CMS apparatus is a superconducting solenoid of 6 m internal diameter, providing a magnetic field of 3.8 T. Within the solenoid volume, there are four subdetectors, including a silicon pixel and strip tracker detector, a lead tungstate crystal electromagnetic calorimeter, and a brass and scintillator hadron calorimeter, each composed of a barrel
and two endcap sections. Iron and quartz-fiber Cherenkov hadron forward (HF) calorimeters,
which are subdivided into 0.175 × 0.175 (∆η × ∆ϕ) towers, cover the pseudorapidity range
2.9 < |η | < 5.2. The silicon tracker measures charged particles within the range |η | < 3.0. For
charged particles with 1 < pT < 10 GeV and |η | < 1.4, the track resolutions are typically 1.5%
in pT and 25–90 (45–150) µm in the transverse (longitudinal) impact parameter [70]. For the
extended pseudorapidity range of charged particles with |η | < 2.4, the track resolutions are
typically 2.5% in pT and 30–200 (150–950) µm in the transverse (longitudinal) impact parameter. A detailed description of the CMS detector, together with a definition of the coordinate
system used and the relevant kinematic variables, can be found in Refs. [71, 72].
The OO and NeNe data were collected in July 2025 and correspond to integrated luminosities
of 7 nb−1 and 0.8 nb−1 , respectively. The events were triggered by requiring one or more energy
deposits of at least 7 GeV in either of the HF calorimeters. In the correlation analysis, an additional requirement is imposed that at least two towers have an energy deposit above 4 GeV in
each of the HF calorimeters. The events are required to contain a single primary vertex [70] (located along the beam axis at zvtx ) that is within 15 cm of the nominal interaction point along the

To isolate initial-geometry effects, the ratios of NeNe to OO vn values are shown in Fig. 2.
The results remain consistent, within uncertainties, before and after correcting for dijet correlations. A significant rise of the v2 ratios from two- and four-particle correlations is observed for
the most central collisions, while the v3 ratios from two-particle correlations exhibit a decreasing trend, which is most pronounced in central events. Unlike the conclusions reached when
examining individual vn values, the Trajectum PGCM calculations reproduce the v2 ratios more
closely, while the NLEFT version tends to overestimate them. Both frameworks qualitatively
capture the observed increase towards central events, consistent with the expectations based
on a deformed geometry of 20 Ne [48]. A quantitative determination of the deformation, however, requires further dedicated studies, since the agreement with data is not uniform across the
measured centrality range. None of the three model calculations reproduce the measured v3 ratios quantitatively, highlighting the need for an improved treatment of initial-state fluctuations
in small systems. The IP-Glasma calculations predict a weaker centrality dependence of the v2
ratio, likely a result of large multiplicity fluctuations that reduce the correlation between centrality and impact parameter [93]. Overall, the precise vn ratios provide stringent constraints
on the modeling of nuclear structure and its implementation in initial-state frameworks, while
underscoring the sensitivity of collective flow to the underlying geometry.
