Abstract
The statistical and squeezing characteristics of the light generated by a two-mode subharmonic generating system in a cavity
pumped by two-mode coherent light and coupled to a two-mode thermal reservoir through a single-port mirror have been
studied in this study. We construct the c-number Langevin equations linked to the normal ordering using the master equation
for the system under discussion, and we establish the correlation characteristics of the noise forces. The mean and variance
of the photon number of the cavity light are then determined using the solutions of the resulting differential equations. We
have discovered that the cavity light mode’s photon statistics are super-Poissonian because the variance of the photon number is more significant than the mean of the photon number. Additionally, using the same solutions, we were able to extract
the antinormally ordered characteristic function, which is used to calculate the Q function. In addition, the photon number
distribution for the cavity mode is computed using the Q function. The variance of the plus and minus quadrature of the
two-mode cavity light generated by the two-mode subharmonic generating system is also obtained. It is discovered that the
cavity mode’s squeezing characteristics are unaffected by the two-mode driving coherent light, however, the thermal light
has the effect of decreasing the squeezing of the cavity light and the squeezing takes place in the plus quadrature. Finally,
we discovered that when the cavity is coupled to a vacuum reservoir, there is a 50% maximum squeezing of the two-mode
cavity at a steady state and at the threshold. The nondegenerate subharmonic generator generates a two-mode cavity light
that is 50% entangled.

characteristics of light produced by various optical systems,
such as lasers, and how light affects atom dynamics [1]. The
quantum states of light, which include the number state,
chaotic state, coherent state, and squeezed state, are well
known [2, 3]. The quantum properties of light are largely
influenced by the state of the light mode. The coherent states
of light are the most significant and recurring in quantum
optics of all the states of light [4]. One source of a two-mode

6 Conclusion
The statistical and squeezing characteristics of the light
produced by a two-mode subharmonic generating system
with a cavity light powered by two-mode coherent light
beams and coupled to a two-mode thermal reservoir via a
single port mirror have been examined in this study. We
determined the c-number Langevin equations for the cavity
mode variables related to the normal ordering by using the

master equation for the system under study and the method
described. We have determined the mean and variance of
the photon number as well as the local mean photon number
for the cavity light using the solutions to these equations.
The mean photon number and photon number variance of
mode a are identical to those of mode b, according to our
findings. We also observed that the variance of the photon
number exceeds the mean photon number of cavity light, and
as a result, the light generated by a nondegenerate subharmonic generator exhibits super Poissonian photon statistics.
Additionally, we have discovered that the thermal reservoir
and the driving coherent light both increase the mean and
variance of the photon number for the cavity mode. Furthermore, we have discovered that for a relatively narrow
frequency range, the local mean photon number approaches
the global mean photon number. Finally, we have calculated
the cavity light’s quadrature variance, quadrature squeezing, and entanglement. Our analysis of the two-mode cavity
light’s quadrature variance reveals that the two-mode cavity
light is squeezed and that the squeezing takes place in the
plus quadrature. Additionally, we have demonstrated that
even if the driving coherent light has no effect on squeezing,
the presence of thermal reservoir has the effect of reducing squeezing, contrary to the parametric interaction, which
enhances squeezing of the two-mode cavity light produced
by our system. Moreover, a two-mode cavity light generated
by a nondegenerate subharmonic generator is 50% entangled.
