Abstract
This article provides an overview of some tentative ideas relating to Planck
scale physics at a level suitable for advanced high school students, beginning
undergraduate students, and teachers. After discussing the ratio of the
electric and gravitational forces, Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle is
combined with Einstein’s theory of special relativity to estimate the scale at
which the strength of gravity becomes comparable to the electrostatic force.
It is shown that the energy required to probe a region of space smaller than
the Planck length may lead to the formation of a black hole, placing a
fundamental lower limit on the distances that can be meaningfully probed in
Nature. The Planck length, mass and time are then obtained using a
combination of dimensional analysis and the fundamental constants
appearing in theories of quantum mechanics, relativity and gravity. Finally, a
heuristic derivation of the generalised uncertainty principle is provided.
Keywords: Planck scale, space, time, quantum mechanics, quantum gravity,
high school, undergraduates
