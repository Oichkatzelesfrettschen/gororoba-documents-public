To study the effect of hydrogen isotope substitution on the uptake of water during formation of clathrate hydrates, the harmonic intermolecular librational modes of selected water clusters (X2 O)n with n = 2–6 and hydrogen isotopes X = H, D, and T are studied. The effects of the
quantum mechanical zero-point energy (ZPE) in each cluster on the binding energies of the H2 O, D2 O, and T2 O clusters are determined, with
ZPE leading to the smallest binding energies in the H2 O clusters and the largest binding energies in the T2 O clusters. Corrections for anharmonicity of the librational modes are considered, and these bring the frequency ranges of the calculated intermolecular librational modes in
the clusters to the experimental ranges of the librational modes in the infrared spectra of H2 O and D2 O solid ice and clathrate hydrate phases,
and liquid H2 O water. These calculations show the expected ranges of the binding energy of tritiated water onto a solid ice and clathrate
hydrate surface and can help quantify the isotopic enrichment on a growing clathrate hydrate phase from the solution.

INTRODUCTION
Isotope effects are the changes in the thermodynamic and
kinetic properties of molecules or bulk phases that result from the
isotopic substitution of atoms in the system.1,2 Isotope effects are
the classical and/or quantum mechanical results of the change in
mass and/or nuclear spin that occur when substituting one or more
atoms in a molecule with its isotopes. Classical effects of isotopic
substitutions arise from the change in mass and its subsequent
effect on the kinetic energy distribution (through the effect on the
center of mass or frequencies of internal vibrational degrees of freedom) of the molecule. Isotopic nuclei manifest differences in nuclear
quantum effects (NQEs), such as differences in zero-point energies
(ZPEs), greater wavefunction delocalization and tunneling rates for
lighter nuclei, and effects on wavefunction symmetry in individual
molecules and bulk phases.2–5

Isotopic substitution of the hydrogen atoms of water increases
the melting point of hexagonal ice I h such that the melting points

substituted waters.

In previous studies, contributions of the ZPE of the intermolecular hydrogen bonding energies of different isotopically substituted
water clusters and bulk phases were not explicitly determined.
The intermolecular hydrogen bonds are associated with librational
modes, which participate in the melting/freezing of ice and formation/dissociation of clathrate hydrate phases. In this work, the
nature of the intermolecular librational modes and how they are
affected by hydrogen isotope exchange in water dimers are also studied. It is useful to evaluate the explicit contributions of the ZPE to
binding energies of isotopically substituted water molecules in the
water clusters, liquid, and ice phases, distinct from other NQEs such
as hydrogen atom wavefunction delocalization, which is described
below.

Quantum mechanical simulations of isotope effects
in water and ice
Quantum mechanical path integral molecular dynamics
(PIMD) simulations5 for liquid water show the combined effects
of all NQEs on the behavior of water. Wavefunction delocalization
is an NQE that affects H atoms to a greater extent than D and T
atoms. The delocalization of the hydrogen atom wavefunction along
the water H–O bond leads to an effective elongation of this bond
and a greater delocalization of the hydrogen atom between two
hydrogen-bonded water oxygen atoms and strengthens the hydrogen bond. The spread of the hydrogen atom wavefunction out of
the O–H⋅ ⋅ ⋅O axis is due to the delocalization of the hydrogen atoms
within the intramolecular H–O–H bending mode. This second effect
weakens the intermolecular hydrogen bonding.10,26–31 PIMD simulations show that these competing effects, along with ZPEs, play
important roles in determining the static and dynamic properties of
liquid water and ice phases under different temperature and pressure conditions, compared to predictions of classical mechanical
simulations.
McBride et al. used path integral Monte Carlo simulations
to incorporate the effects of NQEs on the melting points of D2 O
and T2 O ice using TIP4PQ_D2O/TIP4PQ_T2O empirical potential
models for isotope-substituted water. These potentials are modifications of the TIP4PQ/2005 water potential, which was designed to
effectively reproduce NQEs in classical H2 O water simulations.32
Specifically, in the TIP4PQ/2005 water model, the charge of the
water protons was increased by 0.02e compared to TIP4P/2005
without changing the geometric parameters of the model. In the
TIP4PQ_D2O/TIP4PQ_T2O models, the O–D, O–T, and O–M
bond lengths in the water potential were shortened compared
to the O–H and O–M bond length in the TIP4PQ/2005 model.
This accounts for the experimentally observed shortening of the
O–D and O–T bonds in D2 O and T2 O, respectively, compared to
O–H in H2 O. Using free energy simulations, McBride et al. semiquantitatively reproduced the melting point increase in D2 O and
T2 O ice compared to H2 O ice.32
The accurate analytical MB-pol water–water interaction potential has been developed from high-level ab initio calculations.33–35

water properties. Medders and Paesani used this potential to calculate the infrared and Raman spectra of liquid water using centroid

It was recently demonstrated that during the growth of
clathrate hydrate phases, water molecules in the aqueous phase with
heavier isotopes, in particular T2 O (or mixed species such as THO
and TDO), preferentially bind to the growing hydrate crystal surface, as compared to H2 O water.19,23 In the experiment, an input
water feed of D2 O/H2 O with trace T2 O had an initial radiation
concentration of 4.77 × 105 Bq/kg, which is assigned to a concentration of tritium isotopes in the water molecules. This aqueous
phase was used to grow a clathrate hydrate with HFC-134a (1,1,1,2tetrafluoroethane) as the guest species. After 1 h of HFC-134a
hydrate formation in a semi-batch reactor, the radiation concentration of the remaining liquid aqueous phase was diluted to a final
value of 1.40 × 103 Bq/kg, indicating the preferential attachment of
radioactive T2 O (and other mixed THO and TDO waters) onto the
solid phase during hydrate formation. This method is suggested for
the selective removal of tritiated water from an aqueous environment, a problem encountered in the storage tanks of the Fukushima
Power Plant.
Other experiments similarly indicate the preferential attachment of heavy hydrogen isotope water on solid surfaces. In a beam
experiment, measurements of the ground state sticking probability
of molecular beams of D2 O on D2 O-ice and H2 O-ice show near
unity sticking coefficients, with a 1% isotope effect for increased
sticking of D2 O onto D2 O ice.24 This binding preference was
independent of the vibrational excitation state of the beam D2 O
molecule and is a quantum effect related to the D2 O-surface binding
energy. Shimohata et al. used ab initio calculations to determine that
D2 O has a higher adsorption energy on the Ih(0001) surface than
H2 O.25
The nature of the intermolecular librational modes and their
associated ZPEs is an important contributing factor to the binding of isotopically substituted waters onto a growing ice/clathrate
hydrate surface. In this work, we determine the intermolecular librational modes of clusters of H2 O, D2 O, and T2 O water molecules with
different hydrogen isotopes and the contribution of their ZPEs to
the binding energy of the clusters. The intermolecular librational
modes are usually not localized on water O–H/O–D/O–T bonds,
and the impact of isotope substitution on their frequencies and
ZPEs is not straightforward to determine. ZPEs constitute one of
the contributions to NQEs on intermolecular hydrogen bonding
between water molecules in the condensed liquid water, ice, and
clathrate hydrate phases. By showing that the predicted librational
frequency ranges for the intermolecular binding modes in clusters
are located in similar regions to the intermolecular modes in the
corresponding condensed water phases, the effects of isotopic substitution on the ZPEs of intermolecular binding modes in the water
clusters are expected to be related to those in the water condensed
phases.

of water clusters (H2 O)n from n = 2–6 for H2 O, D2 O, and T2 O
water molecules were conducted at the MP2 level using the aug-ccpVTZ basis set starting from structures given by Babin and Paesani53
and by Temelso and Shields54 with the Gaussian 16 program.55 The
normal mode vibrational analysis for two- and three-water clusters
was also conducted at the MP2/aug-cc-pVQZ level for comparison.
The contributions of the basis set superposition error (BSSE) on
the binding energies of the clusters were determined by comparing with CBS limits calculated by Yoo and Xantheas and Xantheas
et al.39,40 The BSSE is expected to be uniform in comparing the isotope substituted clusters of similar size. Only cyclic structures for the
water trimer, tetramer, and pentamer were considered as Liu et al.
state these are the lowest energy structures.56 Four structures were
considered for the water hexamers.
Harmonic normal mode analysis was conducted on the optimized water clusters to extract the vibrational frequencies and the
zero-point energy. Temelso and Shields used vibrational perturbation theory (VPT2) and calculations at the MP2/aug-cc-pVDZ level
to provide scaling factors to convert the harmonic vibrational ZPE
to anharmonic values in H2 O water clusters.54 They determined
that intermolecular librational modes in clusters are considerably
more anharmonic than intramolecular vibrational modes (primarily
O–H stretch and H–O–H bend modes) and each set of modes can
be assigned a different scaling factor. Including vibrational anharmonicity in the O–H bond stretch of water was required to account
for some experimental results related to the H/D fractionation in
water systems.57

conducted under the Born–Oppenheimer approximation and are

CONCLUSIONS
The semi-classical contributions of the ZPE to the binding
energy E0 of small (H2 O)n , (D2 O)n , and (T2 O)n water clusters are
calculated in this work. The ZPE affects the strength of hydrogen
bonds in clusters and contributes to the NQEs. Including ZPE for
intermolecular vibrational modes increases E0 for D or T isotopically substituted water molecules by ∼1–2 kJ mol−1 compared to H
isotope water in hydrogen bonded water clusters.
The frequencies of intermolecular librational modes for the
H2 O and D2 O clusters considered in this work are in the same
regions as the librational modes in the corresponding solid H2 O
and D2 O ice and clathrate hydrate phases, and some of the structure
motifs of the clusters appear in the condensed water phases. The differences in the ZPE of these isotopically substituted solid phases per
hydrogen bond can be expected to be of the same magnitude as in
the isotopically substituted clusters.
Hydrogen bonding strengths in D2 O water, and by extension
T2 O water, are known to be stronger than those of H2 O water, but D
and T water clusters should show smaller wave function delocalization and tunneling NQEs. PIMD and lattice dynamics simulations
determine that the predicted freezing points of D2 O and T2 O are
higher than those of H2 O but do not directly quantify the separate
contributions to the NQEs.32,37 This study determines the magnitudes of the contribution of the ZPE to binding energies of hydrogen
isotope substituted water clusters. These should be similar to the
ZPE effects of binding of water molecules to solid ice and hydrate
surfaces, which contribute to the different melting points of isotope
substituted ice, dissociation temperatures for the H2 O/D2 O clathrate
hydrate phase, and isotopic enhancement from the liquid phase on
