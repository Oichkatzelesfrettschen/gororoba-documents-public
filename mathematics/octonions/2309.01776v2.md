Octonion algebras over schemes and the equivalence of
isotopes and isometric quadratic forms∗
V. Hildebrandsson†

Abstract
Octonion algebras are certain algebras with a multiplicative quadratic form. In [1], Alsaody
and Gille show that, for octonion algebras over unital commutative rings, there is an equivalence
between isotopes and isometric quadratic forms. The contravariant equivalence from unital
commutative rings to affine schemes leads us to a question: can the equivalence of isometry
and isotopy be generalized to octonion algebras over a (not necessarily affine) scheme? We
present the basic definitions and properties of octonion algebras, both over rings and over
schemes. Then we show that an isotope of an octonion algebra C over a scheme is isomorphic
to a twist by an Aut(C)–torsor. We conclude the paper by giving an affirmative answer to our
question.

Composition algebras over locally ringed spaces were first defined by Petersson in [10]. Since a
locally ringed space (X, OX ) comes with a sheaf of rings OX , the idea is to define the composition
algebra as a pair (C , N ) where C is an OX –algebra that is locally a composition algebra in the ring
sense. Composition algebras only exist in ranks 1, 2, 4, 8, and a composition algebra of rank 8 is
called an octonion algebra.
The structure of the article is as follows. In Section 2, we recall background of sheaf theory over
general sites, the fppf site, and basic notions of octonion algebras over rings. We also present the
main result of Alsaody and Gille. In Section 3, we give the corresponding definitions for octonion
algebras over schemes. In Section 4, we prove several analogous results of those proved in [1, §4 and
§6]. The main results are (1) twisting an octonion algebra C over a scheme by an Aut(C)–torsor
corresponds naturally to an isotope of C, and (2) two octonion algebras over schemes are isotopic
if and only if their quadratic forms are isometric.

Octonion algebras over schemes

In this section, we will present the definition of an octonion algebra over a scheme and some of its
constructions. These definitions will be generalizations of the ones defined in subsection 2.2.
Definition 3.1 (Octonion algebra over a scheme). An octonion algebra over X is a tuple (C, Q)
consisting of
1. a sheaf C of OX -algebras such that for all open affine U ⊂ X, the underlying module of the
OX (U )-algebra C(U ) is projective with constant rank 8,
2. a morphism of sheaves Q : C → OX such that for all open affine U ⊂ X, Q(U ) is a multiplicative, regular, quadratic form on the OX (U )–algebra C(U ). We call Q the quadratic form
on C.
Remark 3.2. In the case where X = Spec(R) is an affine scheme, OX (X) = R, so for an octonion
algebra C over X, C(X) is an R-octonion algebra (as defined in subsection 2.2).
Note that defining the stalks and induced stalk maps (Cx , Qx ) to be octonion algebras over
OX,x , as defined in [10, §1.6 and §1.7], is another natural way to define the octonion algebra over
a scheme. In the following proposition and corollary, we see that the definitions are equivalent.
Proposition 3.3. Let Q : C → OX be a natural transformation. Then, for some open affine
set U ⊂ X, Q(U ) is a regular, multiplicative, and quadratic form if and only if Qx is a regular,
multiplicative, and quadratic form for all x ∈ U .
Proof. We only show the multiplicative part, regularity and being quadratic is similar. The induced
stalk map Qx at x ∈ U can be defined in two steps:
1. Q̃x : (U, s) ↦→ (U, Q(U )(s))
2. Qx : (U, s) ↦→ Q̃x (U, s).
Assume Q(U ) is a regular, multiplicative, and quadratic form. Then, by the definition of Qx for
x ∈ U , it follows that Qx is aswell.
Now, assume for all x ∈ U that Qx is multiplicative. Then Q̃x must be multiplicative. For
a, b ∈ C(U ), we have
(U, Q(U )(ab)) = Q̃x ((U, ab))
= Q̃x ((U, a)(U, b))
= Q̃x ((U, a))Q̃x ((U, b))
= (U, Q(U )(a))(U, Q(U )(b))
= (U, Q(U )(a)Q(U )(b))
so Q(U ) must also be multiplicative. The proof of regularity and being quadratic is similar.
Corollary 3.4. For some open affine set U ⊂ X, (C(U ), Q(U )) is an octonion algebra over OX (U )
if and only if (Cx , Qx ) is an octonion algebra over OX,x for all x ∈ U .
Proof. The rank of C(U ) at x is by definition the rank of Cx , and C(U ) is projective if and only if Cx
is projective for all x ∈ U [6, Exercise 4.11b]. Then the statement follows from proposition 3.3.
