Automorphisms of Sedenions
G. P. Wilmot
University of Adelaide, Australia

arXiv:2512.07210v1 [math.RA] 8 Dec 2025

Abstract
This paper extends the seven-dimensional Fano plane to a 15-dimensional
Fano volume, which is related to sedenions. The Fano plane visualises the
octonions and their structure as seven quaternions and is derived from a
calibration in diﬀerential geometry. Cliﬀord algebra allows the Spin and
Pin groups to fully analyse calibrations and a new calibration is created
that derives the Fano volume, which provides a visualisation of the complete
subalgebra structure of sedenions.
This new 15-dimensional calibration, along with the existing one containing 35 quaternions, enables an explicit derivation of the automorphisms of
sedenions matching Schafer’s result and hence addresses a discrepancy in the
literature. Also, Gresnigt has suggested extending Furey’s work on quark
and lepton families using octonions to three octonion subalgebras of sedenions. The Fano volume allows this process to avoid the power-associative
subalgebras of sedenions. It is conjectured that Fano hyper-volumes will
uncover the complete subalgebra structure of all Cayley-Dickson algebras.
Keywords. Cliﬀord algebras, octonions, sedenions, Cayley-Dickson algebras, calibrations, power-associative algebras, Fano volume.

Summary

Calibrations in Cliﬀord algebra provide a new way to analyse the structure of
Cayley-Dickson algebras and this paper developed maps in order to understand
this relationship. The terms of the co-associative calibrations were called enabling
algebras, because they provide a construction of the automorphisms of CayleyDickson algebras. They uncovered the mechanism for the action of automorphisms,
which is much more complicated in sedenions than in octonions, with enabling
18

## PDF page 19

algebras Spin♯ (7) and Spin♯ (15) as subalgebras of Cl(7) and Cl(15), consisting
of seven commuting 4-forms and 15 commuting 8-forms, respectively, along with
scalars. Quaternions isomorphic to Spin♯ (3) and the map (1) in Cl(3) generates
quaternions so they are self automorphisms in the algebra Cl(3). The even sharp
algebras may be considered as duals of Cayley-Dickson algebras since they have
the same 3-cycle structure of quaternions but are commuting and have squares
that are all unitary.
Cawagas discovered the algebra P4 [12] and found seven copies in sedenions
along with eight octonions [11]. This is only one of three power-associative algebras that exist in Cayley-Dickson algebras with P12 and P14 embedded in doublings
of sedenions. Table 1 summarised how these algebras are part of a structure of
six power-associative, octonion-like algebras and explained the naming convention.
Using a graded notation allows the 15 octonion-like sub-algebras of sedenions to
be uniquely identiﬁed, up to representation and makes obvious the XOR product
structure. These 15 subalgebras were inverse mapped to ﬁnd a new 8-form calibration and Spin♯ (15) in Cl(15) and it is conjectured that this series in inﬁnite.
The original analysis of Brown was partially applied to uncover automorphisms
related to G2 and analysis of the calibration limited this construction to just one
embedding of G2 in S, which agrees with Schafer’s analysis. The automorphisms
of Spin♯ (15) reﬂected the same issues found for S.
Seven of the eight octonions embedded in sedenions that Cawagas found provide
the 15-dimensional G2 and these are easily identiﬁed from the calibration, Φ.
With the 7-dimensional octonion and the seven power-associative subalgebras of
sedenions a Fano volume tetrahedron was constructed containing 35 quaternions
and 15 Fano planes where each quaternion shares three Fano planes and each Fano
plane shares seven quaternions. It is considered possible that Fano hyper-volumes
exist for higher order Cayley-Dickson algebras.
