QUASILINEAR MULTIPLICATION IN THE
CAYLEY–DICKSON TOWER

arXiv:2609.11588v2 [math.RA] 29 Oct 2026

HARRISON LEMLEY
Abstract. Direct evaluation of the defining product of the Cayley–
Dickson algebra An of rank N := 2n over a unital commutative ring
R in which 2 is invertible requires a quadratic number of arithmetic
operations. This paper gives a uniform bilinear algorithm for multiplication in this algebra costing O(N log N ) arithmetic operations in R
and O(N ) auxiliary storage when the doubling parameters of An are
units whose square roots lie in a fixed commutative coefficient algebra
S finitely generated as an R-module and admitting an R-linear map
π : S → R with π(1) = 1. In particular, over R and every finite field
Fq of odd characteristic, a quadratic field extension contains a square
root of every possible doubling parameter, so the algorithm applies to
every Cayley–Dickson tower over these fields, and the tensor rank of
multiplication in An over them is at most (9n − 21)2n + 28 when n ≥ 2.
When the top doubling parameter is a non-square, as in the standard
real tower, the bound is (9n − 15)2n−1 + 10. To the author’s knowledge,
the algorithm presented in this paper is the first uniform subquadratic
algorithm for multiplication in An . When R = R with every doubling
parameter equal to −1, the real quasilinear schedule uses fewer total
arithmetic operations than both direct multiplication and a uniform
algorithm of Cariow–Cariowa for n ≥ 4. An implementation for the
standard real tower is available in the MIT-licensed C11 library fastCD,
with a NumPy-backed Python interface. In single-core benchmarks
against direct multiplication and the uniform Cariow–Cariowa method,
the quasilinear implementation had the lowest mean time of the three at
every tested dimension N ≥ 32, for both single and batched products,
and was over 17 times faster than direct multiplication at N = 1024.

upper bound for the tensor rank of multiplication in An is quadratic in N .

MathOverflow by Kulkov in 2023 [Kul23].
Vector-matrix descriptions of octonionic and related algebras originate
with Zorn, with subsequent developments by Paige and others [Zor31; Pai57;
Suh69; DD99; Gaz26]. The most direct structural precedent here is the

they iterate using the trace-zero subspace with its commutator product and

Cayley–Dickson doubling parameter −1 with their normalized parameter 1.

paper is classical and is not claimed as a contribution of the present work.

The main contribution of this paper is a uniform quasilinear algorithm
obtained by reorganizing the classical recursive structure. As in Karatsuba–
Ofman multiplication [KO62], the improvement comes from reducing the
number of recursive calls, in this case by passing to the alternating product
on imaginary elements. After extending scalars to one fixed commutative
R-algebra S, finitely generated as an R-module and containing the required
square roots, each recursive step uses two half-size recursive calls and lineartime recombination. The scalar–vector products and weighted pairings are
evaluated jointly through a nonrecursive bilinear map, and an R-linear map
recovers R-valued coefficients at the root. The same coefficient algebra is
used throughout, and full multiplication is recovered by scalar arithmetic
and weighted bilinear pairings. For the standard real tower, S = C, and on
real inputs only one initial recursive call is required (Corollary 3.4).
Theorem 1.1. Let R be a unital commutative ring with 2 ∈ R× , and fix
µk ∈ R× for each k ∈ Z>0 . Suppose that a fixed commutative R-algebra S,
finitely generated as an R-module, contains elements ρk satisfying ρ2k = µk
for k ≥ 3 and admits an R-linear map π : S → R with π(1) = 1. For n ≥ 1,
let An be the corresponding Cayley–Dickson algebra, free of rank N = 2n
over R, and let x, y ∈ An . Then multiplication in An can be computed using
O(n2n ) = O(N log N )
arithmetic operations in R, with a sequential evaluation using O(N ) auxiliary
R-coefficients. The algorithm is bilinear, so that every multiplication of
two input-dependent quantities is the product of a fixed R-linear form in the
coordinates of x with a fixed R-linear form in the coordinates of y, with at
most O(N log N ) such multiplications used.
The general arithmetic, bilinear, and storage bounds in Theorem 1.1
follow from Corollaries 4.2 and 4.3 and Proposition 4.5 in Section 4. Since
the algorithm is bilinear, Theorem 1.1 also bounds the bilinear complexity
of multiplication in An , its tensor rank when R is a field, by O(N log N ).
Corollary 4.4 applies this over R and over finite fields of odd characteristic,
where, for n ≥ 2, the bound becomes (9n − 21)2n + 28 in general and
(9n − 15)2n−1 + 10 when µn is a non-square in the field, in particular for
the standard real tower. These bounds come from the general algorithm
with the level-two base case. For the standard real tower, the schedule of
Section 5 reduces the constants further by using an octonion base case when
N ≥ 16. At small n, both schedules use more multiplications than the
fixed-dimensional formulas discussed above.
The accompanying fastCD library provides C and NumPy-backed Python

6. Outlook and future work
The two-call alternating-product recursion presented here gives a uniform
quasilinear method for multiplication throughout the Cayley–Dickson tower
under the hypotheses of Theorem 1.1, together with an explicit O(N log N )
bilinear upper bound and a linear-workspace evaluation. No optimality claim
for the overall multiplication algorithm or matching lower bound is presented.
It remains natural to ask whether the logarithmic overhead can be reduced
and whether further specialized low-dimensional base cases can reduce the
arithmetic constants. Furthermore, the algorithm presented in this paper is
fairly general, applying over a unital commutative ring in which 2 is invertible
and allowing a large range of doubling parameters; it may be fruitful to
investigate whether a more efficient algorithm is possible over specific fields
or rings with more structure to exploit.
In addition to these theoretical questions, several practical matters are likewise left open. A forward-error analysis would clarify the numerical behavior
of the transformed recursion, while cache-aware scheduling and accelerator
implementations could change the observed crossover points. Broader benchmark campaigns are needed before drawing conclusions across hardware or
input distributions. Finally, integrating the present kernel into applications
that already use higher Cayley–Dickson arithmetic would test whether its
isolated multiplication gains translate into end-to-end improvements.
